# Bangkok (กรุงเทพมหานคร) Travel Guide

## Overview

Bangkok, Thailand's capital, overwhelms with sensory intensity: golden temple spires piercing tropical skies, street food smoke curling through humid air, tuk-tuk horns harmonizing with temple bells, and saffron-robed monks gliding through neon-lit streets. It is chaotic, spiritual, delicious, and utterly unforgettable.

## Must-See Attractions

**Grand Palace (พระบรมมหาราชวัง)** -- The dazzling 218-year-old royal complex with ornate halls, golden stupas, and the Emerald Buddha (พระแก้วมรกต) carved from single jade. Wat Phra Kaew within the palace is Thailand's most sacred temple. Dress code strictly enforced: no shorts, sleeveless tops, or exposed skin below knee. Entrance 500 baht; open 8:30-15:30.

**Wat Arun (วัดอรุณ, Temple of Dawn)** -- Striking riverside temple with a 79-meter porcelain-encrusted prang (spire) decorated with broken Chinese ceramics. Climb steep stairs for Chao Phraya River views. Beautiful at sunset when illuminated. Cross river by shuttle boat from Wat Pho (4 baht). Entrance 100 baht.

**Wat Pho (วัดโพธิ์)** -- Home of the 46-meter reclining Buddha covered in gold leaf with mother-of-pearl feet depicting 108 Buddhist symbols. Thailand's oldest and largest temple, also the birthplace of traditional Thai massage. Get a massage inside the temple (260 baht for 30 min, 420 baht for 1 hour). Entrance 200 baht.

**Chatuchak Weekend Market (จตุจักร)** -- 15,000 stalls across 35 acres, one of the world's largest markets. Plants, antiques, fashion, pets, ceramics, food, art. Saturday-Sunday 9:00-18:00, Friday "plant market" day. Arrive before 10 AM to beat heat. Take BTS to Mo Chit station.

**Khao San Road (ถนนข้าวสาร)** -- The legendary backpacker strip: pad thai vendors, reggae bars, massage parlors, fortune tellers, and travelers from every nation. Daytime for souvenir shopping; nighttime for neon-lit party energy. Nearby Soi Rambuttri is quieter but equally atmospheric.

**Floating Markets** -- Damnoen Saduak (2 hours from Bangkok, iconic long-tail boat photos, arrive 7-9 AM), Khlong Lat Mayom (local favorite, fewer tourists, Saturday-Sunday 9-17), and Taling Chan (closest to city center, weekends).

## Hidden Gems

**Jim Thompson House** -- American spy-turned-silk-merchant's traditional Thai house assembled from 6 antique teak buildings. Thompson disappeared in Malaysia in 1967 -- mystery never solved. Silk showroom and garden. Entrance 200 baht.

**Bang Krachao (บางกระเจ่า)** -- Bangkok's "Green Lung," a 7-square-km island of mangrove forests, bicycle paths, and floating houses across the Chao Phraya from Khlong Toei market. Rent bikes at entrance. Sunday farmers' market at Sri Nakhon Khuean Khan Park.

**Erawan Shrine (ศาลเทพารักษ์พระพรหมเอราวัน)** -- Hindu shrine near Grand Hyatt where Thais dance in traditional costumes and offer flowers/incense to the four-faced Brahma statue. Fascinating blend of Hindu-Buddhist practice in Buddhist Thailand.

## Transportation

**BTS Skytrain** -- Two elevated lines (Silom and Sukhumvit) covering central business and tourist areas. 16-59 baht per ride. Day pass 140 baht. Connects to MRT at Sala Daeng/Silom and Asok/Phrom Phong. Runs 6:00-24:00.

**MRT Underground** -- Blue line covers areas BTS doesn't: Hua Lamphong railway station, Chatuchak, Sukhumvit interchange. 16-42 baht. Combined day pass 220 baht for BTS+MRT.

**Chao Phraya Express Boat** -- River bus along the main river. Orange flag boats (16 baht) stop at 30+ piers including Grand Palace, Wat Arun, and Khao San. Tourist boats (150 baht day pass) hit major attractions with English commentary.

**Tuk-Tuks** -- Iconic three-wheeled open-air taxis. Negotiate price BEFORE riding (short trip 50-100 baht, medium 100-200 baht). Avoid tuk-tuks near tourist areas offering "free tours" -- they take you to gem shops with pressure sales. Use only for short, fun rides.

**Taxi** -- Meter required by law. Flag fall 35 baht, distance-based pricing. If driver refuses meter, take another taxi. Tip: say "Meter please" (ช่วยเปิดมิเเตอร์ได้ไหม). Apps: Grab (most reliable, fixed prices).

## Best Seasons

**Cool Season (November-February)** -- The ONLY recommended season. Temperatures 20-32°C, low humidity, minimal rain. Peak tourist season so book hotels early. December-January most comfortable (20-28°C).

**Hot Season (March-May)** -- Brutally hot (34-40°C) with oppressive humidity. April is Thailand's hottest month. Songkran water festival (April 13-15) makes heat bearable with nationwide water fights. Air conditioning essential.

**Rainy Season (June-October)** -- Heavy monsoon rains, usually afternoon downpours 1-2 hours then clearing. Temperatures 28-34°C. Flooding in some streets. Cheaper hotels and fewer tourists. Bring quick-dry clothing and waterproof bags.

## Food & Drink

**Street Food** -- Bangkok's street food is world-renowned. Pad Thai at Thip Samai on Maha Chai Road (legendary, 60-80 baht), som tam (spicy papaya salad) at any roadside cart (40-60 baht), and khao man gai (Hainanese chicken rice) at Go-Ang Pratunam (50 baht).

**Mango Sticky Rice (ข้าวเม่ามะม่วง)** -- The dessert that defines Thai sweetness: ripe mango slices over coconut cream-soaked sticky rice. 80-120 baht at street vendors and markets. Peak season: April-June when mangoes are sweetest.

**Tom Yum Goong (ต้มยำกุ้ง)** -- Thailand's signature soup: spicy, sour, aromatic with lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves, and shrimp. Versions with coconut milk (tom yum nam khon) are richer. 80-150 baht.

**Night Markets** -- Yaowarat (Chinatown) for roasted duck, shark fin soup, and dim sum; Saphan Phut for midnight street food near Memorial Bridge; and Rod Fai (Train Market) for vintage goods plus grilled seafood.

**Thai Ice Tea (ชาเย็น)** -- Orange-colored sweetened tea poured over ice with condensed milk and sometimes star anise or orange blossom water. 25-40 baht everywhere. Sweet, refreshing, addictive.

## Etiquette

- **Temple dress code** -- Cover shoulders and knees. No tank tops, shorts, or revealing clothing at ANY temple. Carry a scarf/light long skirt to slip on. Remove shoes before entering temple buildings.
- **Respect the monarchy** -- Never criticize or joke about the King or royal family. Lèse-majesté laws carry severe penalties (3-15 years imprisonment). Stand respectfully when royal anthems play (before movies and some events).
- **Head and feet** -- The head is sacred (don't touch anyone's head, don't step over someone lying down). Feet are the lowest and dirtiest (don't point feet at people, Buddha images, or monks; don't step on threshold mats).
- **Monks** -- Women must never touch monks or hand objects directly to them (place items on a cloth or table). Men can interact directly. Always give monks priority seating on public transport.
- **No public displays of affection** -- Holding hands is acceptable; kissing and hugging are not Thai norms. Keep displays modest.
- **Wai greeting** -- Press palms together at chest level with slight bow. Higher hands = more respect. Return wais from equals; nod to those younger/lower status who wai you. Never wai to children, servers, or street vendors (just smile and nod).

## Local Saying

« สบายสบาย » (Sabai sabai) -- "Relaxed, comfortable, easy-going." The Thai philosophy of life: don't rush, don't stress, keep cool. When Bangkok overwhelms, remember: sabai sabai.
