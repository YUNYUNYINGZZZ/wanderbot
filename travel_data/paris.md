# Paris Travel Guide

## Overview

Paris, the City of Light (La Ville Lumière), captivates with its elegant architecture, world-class museums, vibrant café culture, and an unmistakable sense of style. From the Gothic towers of Notre-Dame to the sleek glass of the Louvre's pyramid, every street tells a story of art, revolution, romance, and reinvention.

## Must-See Attractions

**Eiffel Tower (Tour Eiffel)** -- Gustave Eiffel's 1889 iron masterpiece, 330 meters tall. Second-floor views are best for photography (€19.50 lift, €11.80 stairs). Visit at night when golden lights sparkle for 5 minutes every hour on the hour. Book online to skip 2-hour queues.

**Louvre Museum (Musée du Louvre)** -- The world's largest museum with 380,000 objects across 3 wings. Mona Lisa, Venus de Milo, and Winged Victory are mandatory, but don't miss the Egyptian antiquities and Napoleon III apartments. Free on first Sundays (Oct-Mar). Allow 4+ hours minimum.

**Musée d'Orsay** -- Impressionist paradise in a converted Beaux-Arts railway station. Monet's water lilies, Van Gogh's self-portraits, Renoir's dance scenes. The café behind the giant clock offers stunning Seine views.

**Montmartre (Sacré-Cœur)** -- The hilltop artists' quarter. Climb to Sacré-Cœur Basilica for panoramic city views, explore Place du Tertre where portrait artists still work, and wander down Rue Lepic where Amélie was filmed. Visit Musée de Montmartre for the studio where Renoir painted Bal du Moulin de la Galette.

**Seine River Cruise** -- 1-hour boats (€15-20) glide past Eiffel Tower, Notre-Dame, Louvre, and Orsay. Bateaux Mouches and Vedettes du Pont Neuf are the two main operators. Evening cruises with dinner (€80+) are magical.

**Notre-Dame Cathedral** -- Still undergoing restoration after the 2019 fire. Exterior viewing possible; full reopening expected 2025-2026. The Île de la Cité island itself is worth exploring -- it's where Paris began as a Roman settlement.

## Hidden Gems

**Promenade Plantée (Coulée Verte)** -- The world's first elevated park, built on a disused railway viaduct 4.5 meters above street level. 4.7 km of gardens, arcades, and artisan workshops below. Inspired New York's High Line.

**Rue Crémieux** -- A tiny pastel-colored street reminiscent of Notting Hill. Instagram darling with lavender, pink, and mint facades. Quiet and residential -- visit respectfully without loud photo sessions.

**Canal Saint-Martin** -- Tree-lined waterway with iron footbridges, trendy cafés, and Sunday strolls. Watch barges navigate the double lock system near Bastille. Great for brunch at Holy Belly or coffee at Ten Belles.

**Parc des Buttes-Chaumont** -- The wildest park in Paris with a suspended bridge, artificial cliff 30 meters high, waterfall, and temple at the summit. Built on a former gypsum quarry and execution ground. Locals' favorite for picnics.

## Day Trip: Versailles

45 minutes by RER C train (€8.50 round trip from Paris). The Château de Versailles features the Hall of Mirrors (357 mirrors reflecting 17 arched windows), Louis XIV's grand apartments, and Marie Antoinette's rustic Hamlet. The 800-hectare gardens with fountains cost €10 extra on Musical Fountain Show days (Saturday/Sunday Apr-Oct).

## Transportation

**Metro** -- 16 lines covering all central Paris. Single ticket €2.15 (paper) or €2.10 (Navigo Easy card). Buy 10-ticket carnet for savings. Lines 1, 4, 14 are fully automated; others have drivers.

**Navigo Découverte Pass** -- Weekly pass (€30 + €5 card fee) for unlimited Metro, bus, RER within zones 1-5 including Versailles and airports. Runs Monday-Sunday (not 7 days from purchase date).

**RER** -- Express suburban trains. RER A connects La Défense business district, RER B connects both airports, RER C reaches Versailles. Shared with Metro within central zones.

**Walking** -- Paris is compact (10 km across). Most attractions within 30-minute walks of each other. Wear comfortable shoes -- cobblestones are beautiful but brutal.

## Best Seasons

**Spring (April-June)** -- Cherry blossoms at Jardin du Luxembourg, lilacs in Tuileries, pleasant temperatures (12-22°C). Fewer crowds than summer. Fashion Week in late January and March brings creative energy.

**Early Autumn (September-October)** -- Golden light on limestone facades, wine harvest festivals, comfortable weather (15-22°C). Parisians return from vacances with renewed energy. Nuit Blanche (all-night art event) in October.

**Summer (July-August)** -- Warm (20-30°C) but many Parisians flee for vacances, leaving quieter streets. Outdoor cinema at Parc de la Villette, concerts at Folies Bergère. Warning: August can be very hot (35°C+); many small restaurants and shops close.

**Winter (November-February)** -- Cold (2-8°C), short days, but magical Christmas lights on Champs-Élysées, holiday markets, and cozy bistro dinners. Musée d'Orsay and Louvre are warm refuges.

## Food & Drink

**Boulangerie Culture** -- French bread is law: the Décret Pain of 1993 defines traditional baguette ingredients (only wheat flour, water, salt, yeast). Look for "Artisan Boulanger" signs. Croissants (€1.20), pain au chocolat (€1.50), and baguette tradition (€1.30) are daily staples.

**Bistrot Dining** -- Classic French comfort food: steak frites (€15-25), duck confit (€18-28), croque monsieur (€10). Lunch menus (menu du midi) at €15-20 offer entrée + plat + dessert. Reserve for dinner.

**Café Etiquette** -- Order a coffee (espresso €2-4) and sit for hours. Terrace seating costs slightly more but is worth it for street-watching. Never rush a café experience. "Un café" means espresso; "un café crème" is a latte.

**Fromageries** -- France has 400+ cheese varieties. Visit a fromagerie (Alléosse on Rue Mouffetard is excellent) and ask for a tasting plate. Comté, Brie de Meaux, Roquefort, and Camembert are classics.

**Wine** -- French wine regions surround Paris: Champagne (1.5 hours), Burgundy (2 hours), Loire Valley (2 hours). In Paris, natural wine bars (caves à vin) like Le Baron Rouge pour affordable glasses from tap dispensers.

## Etiquette

- **Say Bonjour** -- ALWAYS greet with "Bonjour" when entering any shop, restaurant, or encountering anyone. Skipping this is considered extremely rude. Say "Merci, au revoir" when leaving.
- **Dress codes** -- Parisians dress neatly. Avoid athletic wear outside gyms. Smart casual for dinner (no shorts at nice restaurants). Dark colors dominate; bright colors signal "tourist."
- **Tipping** -- Service is included in prices (service compris). Round up or leave €1-2 for good service. 10% for exceptional meals. Never leave 20% -- it's considered excessive and awkward.
- **Restaurant seating** -- Wait to be seated. Don't grab any open table. If the terrace is full, ask "Est-ce qu'il y a une table libre?"
- **Baguette handling** -- Carry baguette openly (never hidden in a bag, it's a cultural offense). Break off pieces rather than cutting. Offer a piece to friends: "Tu veux un morceau?"

## Local Saying

« Fluctuat nec mergitur » -- "She is tossed by the waves but does not sink." Paris's motto since 1853, reflecting the city's resilience through revolutions, wars, and fires.
