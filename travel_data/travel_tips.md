# General Travel Tips

## Visa & Entry Requirements

- Check visa requirements at least 60 days before departure. Rules change frequently.
- Many countries offer e-visas or visa-free entry for short tourist stays (Japan: visa-free for 68 countries up to 90 days; Thailand: visa-free for 56 countries up to 30 days; France: Schengen visa-free for EU/many Western nationals up to 90 days; USA: ESTA for 40 VWP countries, $21, valid 2 years).
- Always carry passport validity exceeding 6 months beyond travel dates -- some countries refuse entry with shorter validity.
- Print or save digital copies of visa approval documents. Border officials may not have real-time access to electronic systems.
- Register with your country's embassy/consulate in the destination country for emergency alerts (US: STEP program; UK: LOCATE; China: 领事直通车 app).

## Packing Essentials

**Universal travel items:**
- Universal power adapter (Type A/B for Americas, Type C/E/F for Europe, Type G for UK/HK)
- Portable charger (10,000+ mAh) -- airports and transit drain phone batteries fast
- Photocopies of passport, visa, insurance, and prescriptions (separate from originals)
- Reusable water bottle (many airports now have filling stations)
- Zip-lock bags for wet clothes, toiletries, and electronics
- Basic first aid: band-aids, antiseptic, pain relievers, motion sickness pills
- Offline map apps downloaded before departure (Google Maps offline areas, Maps.me)

**Seasonal additions:**
- Tropical: quick-dry clothing, UV-protection shirt, insect repellent (DEET 30%+), portable fan
- Winter: thermal base layers, windproof outer layer, hand warmers, lip balm with SPF
- Rainy season: waterproof phone case, quick-dry towel, compact umbrella, waterproof daypack

## Safety

- Register with your embassy before departure for emergency notifications
- Keep emergency numbers: local police, ambulance, fire, and your embassy phone
- Avoid flashing valuables (expensive cameras, jewelry, designer bags) in public
- Use hotel safes for passport (carry photocopy) and excess cash
- Trust instincts: if a situation feels wrong, leave immediately
- Common scams by region:
  - Asia: gem shop tours (forced purchases), taxi overcharging, "closed temple" redirects to alternative shops
  - Europe: pickpocket teams near tourist sites (Eiffel Tower, Rome Colosseum, Barcelona Rambla), fake police asking to "verify" wallet contents
  - Americas: fake petition signers distracting while accomplice picks pocket, ATM skimmers in tourist areas
- Share itinerary with family/friends at home; establish check-in schedule
- Solo female travelers: research destination safety ratings; book women-only hostel dorms; avoid isolated areas after dark

## Money & Banking

- Carry local currency for first 24-48 hours (small vendors, transport, tips often require cash)
- Notify bank of travel dates and countries to prevent fraud alerts blocking cards
- Use ATMs inside banks rather than standalone machines (lower skimmer risk, better assistance if card stuck)
- Visa/Mastercard accepted widely in urban areas; rural areas often cash-only
- Currency exchange at airport for small amounts; better rates at downtown banks or exchange offices
- Budget tracking apps (Trail Wallet, TravelSpend) help monitor daily spending against budget
- Tipping research before arrival: expectations vary dramatically (0% Japan, 5-10% China, 10-15% Europe, 20% USA)

## Health & Insurance

- Purchase travel medical insurance covering emergency evacuation ($50-200 per trip, invaluable for serious incidents)
- Check vaccination requirements 6-8 weeks before travel (some require multiple doses over weeks)
- Carry prescription medications in original labeled containers; bring doctor's letter for customs
- Research local health risks: malaria prophylaxis for tropical regions, altitude sickness medication for high destinations
- Emergency kit: water purification tablets, sunscreen SPF 50+, insect repellent, antihistamines
- Know nearest hospital/clinic locations for each destination; International SOS app provides directories
- Food safety: avoid uncooked street food in regions with poor sanitation; drink bottled/filtered water in developing countries

## Communication

- Download offline translation apps (Google Translate offline packs, Microsoft Translator)
- Get local SIM card or eSIM at airport for data connectivity (essential for maps, translations, ride-hailing apps)
- Popular eSIM services: Airalo (country-specific data packages), Holafly (unlimited data plans)
- WiFi availability: urban centers generally good; rural areas sparse. Cafés and hotels most reliable.
- Emergency communication: embassy contacts saved in phone; ICE (In Case of Emergency) contact in phone settings
- Group travel: establish group chat (WhatsApp/Telegram) for coordination; share live location during separations
