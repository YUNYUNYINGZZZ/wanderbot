# New York City Travel Guide

## Overview

New York City -- The Big Apple, Gotham, the City That Never Sleeps. Five boroughs, 8.3 million people, 800 languages spoken, and an energy that hits you the moment you step out of Penn Station or JFK. From Broadway's neon to Brooklyn's brownstones, NYC is the cultural, financial, and culinary capital of the Western world.

## Must-See Attractions

**Central Park** -- 843 acres of green in the center of Manhattan. Bethesda Fountain, Bow Bridge, Belvedere Castle, Shakespeare Garden, and the Great Lawn. Ice skating at Wollman Rink in winter, rowboats on the Lake in summer. 38 miles of paths; allow a full morning at minimum.

**The Metropolitan Museum of Art (The Met)** -- 2 million works spanning 5,000 years. Egyptian Temple of Dendur (sunset views through the glass wall), European masters, American wing, and the rooftop garden café with Central Park panorama. Pay-what-you-wish for NY/NJ/CT residents; $30 for others.

**Museum of Modern Art (MoMA)** -- Van Gogh's Starry Night, Warhol's Campbell's Soup, Picasso's Les Demoiselles d'Avignon. Free on first Friday evenings (4-8 PM, NYC residents). The design store is excellent for unique gifts.

**Times Square** -- The crossroads of the world at Broadway and 42nd-47th Streets. 50+ giant LED billboards, 300,000 daily pedestrians, and New Year's Eve ball drop. Best at night when the neon takes over. TKTS booth offers same-day Broadway discounts up to 50%.

**Brooklyn Bridge** -- Walk the 1.8 km pedestrian promenade from Manhattan to Brooklyn for the classic skyline photo. Start at dawn or sunset for dramatic light. Continue into DUMBO for waterfront parks and Jane's Carousel ($3).

**9/11 Memorial & Museum** -- Twin reflecting pools marking the footprints of the original towers, surrounded by 400+ swamp white oak trees. The museum chronicles the day with artifacts, recordings, and personal stories. Free memorial; museum $33.

**Broadway** -- 41 theaters producing the world's premier live entertainment. Hamilton, The Lion King, Wicked, and emerging hits. Rush tickets ($40) available morning of show day for students. TodayTix app offers mobile rush lotteries.

## Hidden Gems

**The High Line** -- 2.3 km elevated park on a former freight rail line on Manhattan's West Side. Gardens, art installations, food vendors, and views into Chelsea apartments. Access at Gansevoort, 14th, 16th, 23rd, or 30th Streets.

**Greenwich Village (The Village)** -- Bohemian birthplace of Bob Dylan, Beat poets, and LGBTQ+ rights (Stonewall Inn, now a National Monument). Tree-lined streets, jazz clubs (Village Vanguard since 1935), and independent bookstores (Strand: 18 miles of books).

**Roosevelt Island Tramway** -- Aerial tram gliding over the East River from Manhattan to Roosevelt Island in 4 minutes ($2.90 MetroCard). Spectacular views of Manhattan skyline. The island's FDR Four Freedoms Park is an architectural masterpiece.

**Flushing Meadows-Corona Park (Queens)** -- The Unisphere (12-meter steel globe from 1964 World's Fair), Queens Museum (panorama of entire NYC), and diverse food courts representing 100+ nationalities. A true global crossroads.

## Transportation

**Subway** -- 472 stations, 28 lines, 24/7 operation. $2.90 per ride with OMNY (tap phone/card) or MetroCard. 7-day unlimited MetroCard $34. Express trains skip local stops; check signs carefully. Avoid empty cars late at night.

**Bus** -- $2.90, same fare as subway with free transfer within 2 hours. Useful for crosstown travel where subway lines run north-south. Select Bus Service (SBS) requires pre-payment at street kiosks.

**Taxi/Uber/Lyft** -- Yellow taxis: $2.50 base + $0.50 per 1/5 mile + surcharges. Uber/Lyft competitive for longer trips. Cars are right-turn hazards -- always look both ways crossing streets.

**Walking** -- Manhattan grid makes navigation simple: numbered streets run east-west, avenues north-south. Walk 20 blocks north-south = ~1 mile. 10 blocks east-west = ~0.5 mile. Broadway is the diagonal exception.

## Best Seasons

**Autumn (October-November)** -- Crisp air (10-18°C), foliage in Central Park, Halloween parade in The Village, and Macy's Thanksgiving Parade. Cultural season launches: opera, ballet, theater premieres.

**Spring (May-June)** -- Blooming cherry trees at Brooklyn Botanic Garden, outdoor dining begins, pleasant temperatures (15-25°C). Fleet Week brings Navy ships. Less crowded than summer.

**Summer (July-August)** -- Hot and humid (25-35°C), but free outdoor concerts (SummerStage, MoMA Nights), beach at Coney Island, and rooftop bars. Many locals escape to Hamptons or Hudson Valley weekends.

**Winter (December-February)** -- Cold (0-5°C) but magical: Rockefeller Center tree, holiday windows on 5th Avenue, Bryant Park winter village with free ice skating and holiday shops. Restaurant Week in January (3-course lunches $29, dinners $49).

## Food & Drink

**Pizza** -- NYC invented the thin-crust foldable slice. Joe's Pizza (Greenwich Village), Di Fara (Midwood, Brooklyn -- the holy grail), and Prince St. Pizza (SoHo pepperoni square slice). $3-5 per slice.

**Bagels** -- Ess-a-Bagel (Midtown), Russ & Daughters (Lower East Side, 1914), and Tompkins Square Bagels (East Village). Cream cheese (called "a schmear"), lox, and capers. $3-5 per bagel.

**Street Food** -- Halal Guys (53rd & 6th, chicken/rice platter $8), dumplings in Chinatown ($1 for 5 at Shu Jiao Fu Zhou), and pretzels/cashews from corner carts ($2-3). Food trucks gather at SoHo and Flatiron.

**Restaurants** -- 27,000+ restaurants across every cuisine. Reservations via Resy or OpenAI apps. Prix fixe menus at Michelin stars: Le Bernardin (3-star, seafood), Eleven Madison Park (3-star, vegan since 2021), and Gramercy Tavern (1-star, seasonal American).

**Coffee** -- Third-wave coffee revolution: Blue Bottle, La Colombe, Devocion (fresh-roasted Colombian beans). Espresso bars everywhere. Tip $1 per drip coffee, $2 for specialty drinks.

## Etiquette

- **Tipping** -- Mandatory culture. 20% for restaurants (18% minimum, 22% for excellent service). $1-2 per drink at bars. 15-20% for taxis. 10-15% for haircuts. Not tipping is deeply offensive and may be confronted.
- **Pace** -- NYC moves fast. Walk purposefully, don't block sidewalks (stand to the side to text), keep moving on escalators (stand right, walk left). Restaurant waits are normal; don't complain.
- **Jaywalking** -- Universal practice. Cross when traffic clears regardless of signal. But watch for cars turning right on red and bikes in bike lanes. Pedestrians have right of way but assert it carefully.
- **Street interaction** -- Don't engage with aggressive panhandlers or Times Square "free CD" promoters. Keep walking. "No thank you" firmly is acceptable. Ask police officers or store clerks for directions.
- **Subway** -- Let passengers exit before boarding. Don't block doors. Stand clear of closing doors. Backpacks off during rush hour. Offer seats to elderly, pregnant, disabled riders.

## Local Saying

"If you can make it here, you can make it anywhere." -- Frank Sinatra, anthem of NYC ambition. The city tests everyone, but those who persevere find unmatched opportunity.
