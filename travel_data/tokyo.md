# Tokyo (東京) Travel Guide

## Overview

Tokyo, Japan's sprawling capital, merges ultramodern architecture with traditional temples, neon-lit entertainment districts with serene gardens, and cutting-edge technology with centuries-old craftsmanship. It is a city of contrasts where you can wander from a 7th-century shrine to a robot café within minutes.

## Must-See Attractions

**Senso-ji Temple (浅草寺)** -- Tokyo's oldest temple in Asakusa, dating to 645 AD. Enter through the iconic Kaminarimon (雷門) gate with its giant red lantern, stroll down Nakamise-dori shopping street for traditional snacks and crafts, and experience the temple's vibrant festivals.

**Meiji Shrine (明治神宮)** -- A peaceful Shinto shrine nestled in a 170-acre forest of 100,000 trees in the heart of Harajuku. Written prayers (ema) can be left at the inner hall. The forest was hand-planted, each tree donated by a different Japanese citizen.

**Shibuya Crossing** -- The world's busiest intersection where up to 3,000 people cross simultaneously when the light turns green. Best viewed from the Starbucks above or the Shibuya Sky observation deck. The statue of Hachiko, the loyal dog who waited 9 years for his deceased owner, stands nearby.

**Tsukiji Outer Market (築地場外市場)** -- While the inner wholesale market moved to Toyosu in 2018, the outer market remains a vibrant food lover's paradise. Fresh sushi, tamagoyaki (sweet rolled omelet), and oden (winter hot pot) are sold from tiny stalls starting at 5 AM.

**Akihabara (秋葉原)** -- Electric Town, the epicenter of anime, manga, gaming, and electronics culture. Multi-floor arcades, maid cafés, retro game shops, and figure stores fill every block. Don't miss Super Potato for classic Famicom and Sega games.

**teamLab Borderless / Planets** -- Immersive digital art museums where visitors walk through flowing light, floating flowers, and infinite crystal universes. Book tickets weeks in advance; they sell out fast.

## Hidden Gems

**Gotokuji Temple (豪徳寺)** -- The origin of the maneki-neko (beckoning cat). Hundreds of cat figurines cover the temple grounds, each placed by visitors whose wishes came true. A quiet, magical spot far from tourist crowds.

** Yanaka Ginza (谷中銀座)** -- A preserved shitamachi (old downtown) shopping street with cat-shaped snacks, traditional sweet shops, and a slower pace of life. Walk from Nippori Station through Yanaka Cemetery (where Tokugawa shogunate family rests) to reach it.

**Odaiba Palette Town** -- A waterfront entertainment island with a half-scale Statue of Liberty, Fuji TV's bizarre golden sphere building, and the Digital Art Museum. Great evening views of Rainbow Bridge.

## Day Trip: Hakone (箱根)

Just 90 minutes from Shinjuku by Romancecar train. Volcanic hot springs (onsen), views of Mt. Fuji from Owakudani's black egg-boiling sulfur vents, open-air sculpture museum, and a pirate ship cruise across Lake Ashi. The Hakone Free Pass covers all transport.

## Transportation

**JR Pass** -- The 7-day Japan Rail Pass (¥50,000) covers all JR lines nationwide including most Shinkansen. Purchase the pass voucher BEFORE arriving in Japan; exchange at major stations. For Tokyo-only travel, a pass is not worth it -- individual fares are cheap.

**Suica/Pasmo IC Cards** -- Rechargeable smart cards for trains, buses, convenience stores, and vending machines. Tap in and out at gates. Buy at any station kiosk. ¥500 deposit, refundable at JR stations when leaving.

**Tokyo Metro** -- 13 subway lines covering every district. Last trains around midnight, first trains around 5 AM. Avoid rush hours (7:30-9:30 AM) when trains run at 200% capacity.

## Best Seasons

**Spring (March-April)** -- Cherry blossom (sakura 桜) season, Japan's most celebrated natural event. Peak bloom in Tokyo typically late March to early April. Ueno Park, Shinjuku Gyoen, and Meguro River are top hanami spots. Weather is mild (12-20°C) with occasional rain.

**Autumn (November)** -- Brilliant koyo (紅葉) autumn foliage at Rikugien Garden, Meiji Jingu Gaien, and Mount Takao. Temperatures comfortable (10-18°C), lower tourist crowds than spring.

**Summer (July-August)** -- Hot and humid (25-35°C), but packed with festivals: Sumida River fireworks, Bon Odori dances, and tanabata decorations. Beat the heat at underground shopping arcades and morning markets.

**Winter (December-February)** -- Cold but clear (2-10°C), excellent for hot spring visits, illumination events (Caretta Shiodome, Marunouchi), and New Year temple visits (hatsumode).

## Food & Drink

**Ramen (ラーメン)** -- Tokyo offers every style: shoyu (soy sauce) at local shops, tonkotsu (pork bone) at Fuunji in Shinjuku, and tsukemen (dipping noodles) at Rokurinsha. Expect ¥800-1,200 per bowl. Lunch queues at famous shops start at 11 AM.

**Sushi (寿司)** -- From conveyor-belt kaiten-zushi (¥100-300 per plate) to omakase at Ginza masters (¥20,000+). Mid-range options at Tsukiji Outer Market offer superb quality at ¥2,000-4,000 per set.

**Tempura (天ぷら)** -- Light, crispy battered seafood and vegetables. Asakusa's Daikokuya serves Edomae-style tempura since 1887.

**Convenience Store Culture** -- 7-Eleven, Lawson, and FamilyMart are culinary institutions. Onigiri (rice balls ¥120), egg sandwiches (tamago sando ¥250), and fried chicken (kara-age) are surprisingly excellent. Lawson's "Premium Roll" cake is legendary.

** izakaya (居酒屋)** -- Japanese gastropubs with small dishes and beer/shochu. Order oden, edamame, yakitori, and kara-age. Expect ¥3,000-5,000 per person for a full evening.

## Etiquette

- **No tipping** -- Tipping is considered rude and confusing. Good service is the standard, not something to be rewarded extra.
- **Bow** -- A slight bow (15°) for casual greetings, deeper bow (30°) for respect. Handshake is also acceptable for foreigners.
- **Train silence** -- Phones on silent mode, no phone calls on trains. Conversations are quiet. Priority seats near doors should be vacated for elderly and disabled passengers.
- **Chopstick rules** -- Never stick chopsticks upright in rice (resembles funeral incense). Never pass food chopstick-to-chopstick (resembles a funeral ritual). Use chopstick rests when pausing.
- **Shrine etiquette** -- Wash hands and mouth at the chozuya water pavilion before entering. Bow twice, clap twice, bow once when praying. Walk on the sides of the approach path; the center is for the gods.
- **Shoes off** -- Remove shoes when entering homes, temples, ryokan, and some restaurants. Toe-facing-outdoor placement signals you're leaving; toe-facing-indoor signals you're staying.

## Local Saying

「一期一会」 (Ichi-go ichi-e) -- "One time, one meeting." Every encounter is unique and will never be repeated in exactly the same way. Cherish each moment.
